# Principles of Accounts instructional review

Reviewed against the supplied CXC 10/G/SYLL 17 syllabus and its 106 specific objectives. This review concerns the original teaching notes in `src/principlesOfAccounts/data/poaLessons.js`. It does not certify historical answer keys, live marking, or publication readiness.

The course retains 33 lessons in 11 sections. Every objective remains mapped to exactly one lesson. Mapping alone is not evidence of mastery; the expansion adds practical reasoning and calculations where earlier notes were brief.

The closing-capital lesson also includes a clearly labelled extension for estimating profit from opening/closing capital when records are incomplete. Incomplete-record reconstruction is not presented as an additional specific objective in this syllabus, and the extension does not claim to reconstruct all missing accounts.

## Deliberate boundaries

- Payroll rates are illustrative exercise inputs. The notes do not assert current territory-specific tax or social-security rates.
- Software names are explicitly identified as examples supplied by the syllabus, without claims about current editions or regional payroll support.
- Dividend and cooperative distribution examples state or depend on supplied terms; they do not invent mandatory legal reserve or dividend policies.
- The syllabus requires preparation of receipts and payments accounts for clubs. Income and expenditure is identified and explained; it is not falsely described as an additional preparation requirement here.
- LIFO is taught for the supplied syllabus. Teaching its calculations does not assert that every reporting framework permits it.

## Work outside this notes review

Historical paper transcription, official answer-key verification, method-mark calibration and production publishing still need their own evidence. These narrative notes and their numerical examples do not substitute for those checks. The interactive course should continue to expose worked examples, common mistakes and self-checks clearly, and historical grading should remain distinguishable from original practice.
